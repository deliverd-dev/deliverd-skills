# Executive summary

One page. Read in two minutes, by someone who will not read the appendix and
should not have to.

## Shape

1. **The decision or finding**, in one sentence, in the largest type on the
   page.
2. **Three supporting points.** Not five. If there are five, two are detail.
3. **The numbers that matter**, three or four, with their comparisons.
4. **What happens next** — the ask, the owner, the date.
5. **Where the detail lives**, as a link. This is what makes one page
   defensible.

## Rules

- It fits on one screen at 1280×800 and on one sheet of A4. If it does not,
  it is a report — write one of those and summarise it.
- No jargon that the reader would have to look up, and no acronym unexpanded
  at first use.
- The ask is explicit. "We recommend approving X by the 14th" beats "there are
  several options to consider", which reads as having done the analysis and
  declined to conclude.
- Uncertainty stated plainly and briefly. "Based on eight weeks of data;
  seasonal effects not yet separable." One sentence, not a section.
- Style it for print: this one gets printed. See the print block in
  `craft.md`.

## Common failure

Hedging until there is no recommendation left. An executive summary that
refuses to conclude has wasted the page — the reader came for the conclusion
and can find the caveats in the report it links to.
