# Report

A written argument the reader is expected to act on. The longest form here,
and the one most often ruined by being organised the way the work happened
rather than the way it needs to be read.

## Shape

1. **Title and standfirst.** What this is, what period it covers, one sentence
   on what it found. Date it.
2. **The finding, in full, first.** Not "background". If the reader stops
   here they should still be able to act correctly.
3. **Evidence**, one section per claim, heading naming the claim.
4. **What it means** — the interpretation, kept separate from the evidence so
   a reader can disagree with one and not the other.
5. **Recommendation**, with an owner and a date if you have them.
6. **Method and caveats**, last. Sample size, what was excluded, what you
   estimated.

## Rules

- Every number carries a period and a unit at first use. "Revenue" is not a
  number; "€8.4m, Q2 2026" is.
- A comparison needs its baseline in the same sentence. "Up 12%" against what?
- Label anything inferred, modelled or estimated **in the document**. A caveat
  that lives only in the chat message does not survive being forwarded.
- Prose in paragraphs. A report that is entirely bullet points is a set of
  notes, and reads as though nobody was willing to commit to a sentence.
- Tables for anything with more than three comparable items.
- Body text at `max-width: 68ch`, generous leading. This will be read properly,
  not skimmed.

## Common failure

Burying the conclusion under context, because that is the order in which the
analysis was done. Write the sections in whatever order you like; put the
answer at the top before you finish.
