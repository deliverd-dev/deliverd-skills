Delivery slipped in Q2, and it was one region

On-time delivery fell to 91.4% across the quarter, from 94.2% in Q1. Three quarters of the shortfall came from the Northern depot, where a single unreplaced vehicle removed roughly 40 drops a day from capacity.

1 April – 30 June 2026 · Prepared 4 July 2026 · Source: dispatch system, extracted 2 July

The fix is a vehicle, not a process. Northern ran at 82% on-time against a network average of 93.6% once it is excluded. Its per-drop times, route density and staffing are unchanged from Q1. The depot lost a 3.5t van on 14 April and has been covering with overtime since.

Northern accounts for three quarters of the gap

Every other depot finished the quarter within 1.2 points of its Q1 figure. Northern moved 11.4 points. Weighted by volume, it contributes 2.1 of the 2.8-point network decline.

What it means

Overtime has held volume up: Northern completed 3.1% more drops than in Q1 while missing more windows. That is the shape of a capacity shortfall being absorbed by hours rather than by vehicles, and it explains the 19p rise in cost per drop almost exactly.

It is not a scheduling or a staffing problem. Route density, average drop time and headcount are all within a point of Q1.

Recommendation

Replace the Northern van before the end of July. On Q2 volumes it returns an estimated 2.0 points to the network figure and removes about £34,000 of quarterly overtime — against a lease cost of roughly £9,000 a quarter.

Method and caveats

On-time is a drop completed inside its promised window, from the dispatch system, excluding 1,840 drops (1.0%) where the window was missing. The overtime and lease figures are estimates from Q2 run rates, not quotes. Seasonal effects are not separable with only five quarters of comparable data.
